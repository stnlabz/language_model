Purpose

The Security Research Threat (SRT) process establishes a structured method for documenting, investigating, reviewing, and preserving potential security conditions identified through STN-LABZ research, development, intelligence, or operational activity.

An SRT allows a potential threat to be investigated without prematurely establishing that the reported condition is true, exploitable, applicable to STN-LABZ, or an established organizational threat.

Identification

A potential Security Research Threat may be identified through:

- STN-LABZ research and development.
- Rictus intelligence collection.
- News and intelligence aggregation.
- Engineering observations.
- Security research.
- Operational observations.
- Other authorized STN-LABZ activities.

Rictus may identify and report an SRT candidate.

Identification of an SRT candidate does not establish the reported condition as fact.

Security Research Threat Record

Each SRT will use a stable identifier:

SRT-YYYY-MM-DD

The SRT record will include, as applicable:

Title / Research State

Identify the subject of the research and its current research state.

Threat

What potential security condition has been identified?

Observation

What has actually been observed, demonstrated, or reported?

Observation will remain distinguishable from interpretation or inference.

Potential Impact

How could the identified condition affect STN-LABZ systems, operations, engineering, security, infrastructure, or the public?

Affected Domains

Applicable domains may include:

- Desktop / Laptop
- Server
- Network
- Space
- AI
- Communications
- Infrastructure
- Other affected systems or environments

Evidence

Preserve evidence supporting the SRT, including as applicable:

- Original discovery source.
- Root or primary source.
- Supporting references.
- Technical documentation.
- Research.
- Demonstrations.
- Incidents.
- Contradictory evidence.
- Other relevant evidence.

Provenance will be preserved sufficiently to allow later verification.

Assessment

Analysis and interpretation will remain distinguishable from established facts.

Capability will not be interpreted as authority.

The existence of a technical capability does not establish that its use was authorized, intended, exploitable, or applicable to STN-LABZ.

Unknowns

Information that cannot be established from available evidence will remain UNKNOWN.

Unknown information will not be replaced with speculation.

Confidence

Confidence will be identified as:

- LOW
- MODERATE
- HIGH

Confidence describes the strength of the available assessment. It does not convert an unverified condition into established fact.

Recommended Research

Identify additional investigation, testing, evidence, or analysis required to determine the condition more completely.

Potential Mitigation

Potential defensive or engineering measures may be documented for research and review.

Potential mitigation is not policy unless separately approved through the STN-LABZ policy process.

Research State

An SRT candidate will initially be treated as:

POTENTIAL — NOT ESTABLISHED

The condition remains potential until sufficient evidence and authorized human review establish otherwise.

Human Review

SRT candidates require human review.

Human review may result in the candidate being:

- Approved for further research.
- Revised.
- Escalated.
- Closed.

Creation of an SRT candidate does not establish that:

- The reported condition is true.
- The condition is exploitable.
- The condition applies to STN-LABZ.
- The condition constitutes an established organizational threat.
- The condition requires policy.
- The condition has been approved as a research assignment.

Human authorization is required before an SRT candidate becomes an approved research assignment.

Disposition

An SRT may receive an applicable disposition including:

- MONITOR
- RESEARCH
- ESCALATE
- RECOMMEND POLICY
- CLOSED

Disposition will reflect the current authorized treatment of the SRT and will not be interpreted as evidence beyond what the SRT record establishes.

Policy Boundary

An SRT does not create organizational policy.

An SRT may recommend policy when research identifies a condition requiring organizational governance.

Policy creation, amendment, approval, or rejection remains a separate human-governed process.

Potential mitigation documented within an SRT does not become mandatory merely because it appears in the research record.

Authority Boundary

Security research identifies and evaluates potential conditions.

Rictus may discover, collect, correlate, document, and report information relevant to an SRT candidate within its assigned mission.

Rictus will not:

- Independently establish an SRT candidate as an organizational threat.
- Independently approve SRT research assignments.
- Independently approve organizational knowledge.
- Establish company policy.
- Bypass required human review.
- Expand its own authority.

Human authority remains the approval boundary.

Historical Record

SRT records will preserve supporting and contradictory evidence necessary to understand how the assessment developed.

An SRT that is rejected, disproven, revised, or closed remains part of the engineering and security research record.

Contrary evidence will not be removed merely because it weakens the original hypothesis.
