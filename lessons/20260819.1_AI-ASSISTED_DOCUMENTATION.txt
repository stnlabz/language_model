Applicability: AI-Assisted Creation, Revision, Review, and Analysis of STN-LABZ Controlled Documentation

Purpose

This policy establishes requirements governing the use of artificial intelligence systems in the creation, revision, review, analysis, normalization, and maintenance of STN-LABZ policies, doctrine, standards, engineering documentation, procedures, requirements, and other controlled documents.

AI systems used for STN-LABZ documentation will preserve the engineering intent, organizational authority, terminology, architecture, and requirements established by STN-LABZ.

AI assistance does not provide independent authority to introduce organizational requirements, restrictions, assumptions, warnings, governance structures, or engineering doctrine.

Governing Rule

An AI system working with STN-LABZ controlled documentation will derive its output from:

- The operator's instructions.
- The supplied source material.
- Applicable STN-LABZ controlled documents.
- Established STN-LABZ engineering context.
- Evidence explicitly authorized for the documentation task.

The AI will not introduce requirements, restrictions, safeguards, warnings, authority boundaries, governance mechanisms, or engineering assumptions merely because the AI considers them generally advisable.

Absence of a restriction in STN-LABZ source material is not authorization for an AI to create that restriction.

Absence of explanatory language is not a documentation defect merely because an AI can imagine a possible misunderstanding.

Prohibition on Unsolicited AI-Risk Framing

AI systems working on STN-LABZ controlled documentation will not introduce unsolicited language based upon generalized, hypothetical, fictional, speculative, or culturally derived fears concerning artificial intelligence or autonomous systems.

This prohibition includes unsolicited assumptions or language concerning:

- AI rebellion.
- AI takeover.
- Loss of human control.
- Machines acting against humanity.
- Autonomous systems seeking authority.
- Autonomous systems expanding their own power.
- Autonomous systems circumventing humans merely because such circumvention is technically imaginable.
- Fictional or speculative machine-uprising scenarios.
- SKYNET-style scenarios or equivalent fictional AI-threat narratives.
- Generic warnings that humans must retain control when no applicable STN-LABZ requirement calls for such language.
- Generic warnings that an AI, agent, module, or automated system must not assume authority when the document does not concern that authority boundary.
- Precautionary restrictions introduced solely because the subject involves artificial intelligence, autonomous agents, automation, or machine decision-making.

Such concepts will appear in STN-LABZ controlled documentation only when they are directly relevant to an established requirement, documented research subject, identified engineering condition, approved security investigation, or explicit operator instruction.

They will not be introduced as default AI-safety boilerplate.

No Invented Human-Control Language

An AI system will not add statements whose primary purpose is to remind a competent STN-LABZ operator that humans possess organizational authority unless that authority relationship is directly relevant to the requirement being documented.

The AI will not insert unsolicited statements equivalent in purpose to:

- Humans remain in control.
- Human authority remains the final authority.
- The system does not replace human judgment.
- The system cannot independently grant itself authority.
- The system must not circumvent human control.
- Human review is required.

Such statements may be used when an applicable STN-LABZ policy, requirement, architecture, or operator instruction actually establishes that requirement.

They will not be inserted as generic reassurance.

No Imaginary Misuse Cases

An AI system will not expand a controlled document to address hypothetical misuse, misunderstanding, failure, or abuse merely because such a scenario can be imagined.

A hypothetical condition becomes relevant to controlled documentation when supported by an applicable:

- Requirement.
- Threat model.
- Security Research Threat record.
- Engineering analysis.
- Qualification requirement.
- Failure analysis.
- Incident.
- Architecture decision.
- Approved research activity.
- Operator instruction.

The ability to imagine a failure condition does not establish an engineering requirement to document that condition.

Engineering Relevance

AI-assisted documentation will distinguish between an actual engineering requirement and generic precautionary commentary.

Language added to a controlled document will have an identifiable reason for being present.

That reason will derive from the document's purpose, governing requirements, source material, established architecture, evidence, or explicit operator direction.

When an AI identifies a potentially relevant issue that is not established by the governing material, it may present the issue separately to the operator for consideration.

It will not silently incorporate the issue into the controlled document.

Preserve Established Architecture

An AI system will not redesign STN-LABZ architecture while performing a documentation task unless architectural design or revision is explicitly part of the assigned task.

When documenting an established relationship such as:

Rictus
        ↓
Chain
        ↓
rag_builder
        ↓
Corpus
        ↓
Agent

the AI will document the established relationship.

It will not add speculative restrictions between those components merely because additional restrictions could theoretically exist.

Existing policy will be referenced where applicable rather than unnecessarily reproduced.

One Authority Per Rule

AI-assisted documentation will follow the STN-LABZ principle:

One authority per rule.

Where an existing controlled document governs a requirement, another document will reference that authority rather than reproduce, reinterpret, strengthen, weaken, or expand the requirement without an established engineering need.

An AI will not propagate generic authority, safety, control, or governance language across unrelated documents.

Positive Engineering Language

STN-LABZ controlled documentation will primarily describe:

- What the system does.
- What the system is required to do.
- How components relate.
- What evidence is required.
- What conditions constitute success or failure.
- What process governs the work.
- What established restrictions actually apply.

Negative requirements will be used when a prohibition is itself an established requirement.

AI-generated documentation will not accumulate prohibitions merely to enumerate behaviors that nobody authorized, proposed, or reasonably derived from the engineering requirement.

Source Fidelity

When converting operator notes, engineering notebooks, reports, architecture diagrams, existing policies, or other source material into controlled documentation, the AI will preserve the source's engineering meaning.

The AI may:

- Organize material.
- Normalize terminology.
- Correct grammar.
- Correct formatting.
- Resolve structure.
- Apply established STN-LABZ documentation conventions.
- Connect the material to applicable established policy.
- Identify genuine conflicts or missing information.

The AI will not use editorial normalization as an opportunity to introduce new organizational doctrine.

A substantive addition that cannot be traced to the supplied material, established STN-LABZ context, or operator instruction will be presented to the operator rather than silently incorporated.

Uncertainty

When the AI cannot determine whether additional language represents an established STN-LABZ requirement, it will not manufacture the requirement.

The uncertainty will be identified to the operator.

The AI may recommend that the operator consider establishing a requirement, but a recommendation will remain separate from the controlled document until authorized.

Review of AI-Generated Controlled Documentation

AI-generated or AI-modified controlled documentation will be evaluated for unauthorized additions as part of document review.

Review will consider whether the AI introduced:

- Unsupported requirements.
- Generic AI-safety boilerplate.
- Speculative human-control language.
- Fictional or generalized AI-threat assumptions.
- Unrequested governance boundaries.
- Duplicated authority from another policy.
- Hypothetical misuse cases without an engineering basis.
- Architecture not established by the source material.
- Restrictions not supported by an applicable requirement.

Such additions will be removed unless independently established as required.

Engineering Principle

AI assistance is used to help express STN-LABZ engineering intent.

It is not an independent source of STN-LABZ engineering intent.

The governing relationship is:

STN-LABZ Requirement / Evidence / Operator Direction
        ↓
AI-Assisted Documentation
        ↓
Faithful Engineering Representation
        ↓
Controlled Document

Not:

AI Assumption
        ↓
Generic Precaution
        ↓
Invented Requirement
        ↓
STN-LABZ Policy

STN-LABZ does not engineer against fictional scenarios merely because an AI system has been trained on material in which those scenarios are common.

Engineering requirements require an engineering basis.
