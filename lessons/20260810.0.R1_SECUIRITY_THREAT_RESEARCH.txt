Purpose

The Security Research Threat (SRT) process establishes a structured,
human-controlled method for identifying, documenting, investigating,
reviewing, preserving, and, when authorized, publishing potential
security conditions identified through STN-LABZ research, development,
intelligence, or operational activity.

An SRT allows a potential security condition to be investigated without
prematurely establishing that the reported condition is true, exploitable,
applicable to STN-LABZ, or an established organizational threat.

The SRT process preserves the distinction between discovery, research,
assessment, human decision, publication, and organizational knowledge.

Completion of one SRT processing stage does not independently authorize
progression to another stage.

Human interaction remains required at applicable authority boundaries.


Identification

A potential Security Research Threat may be identified through:

- STN-LABZ research and development.
- Rictus intelligence collection.
- News and intelligence aggregation.
- Engineering observations.
- Security research.
- Operational observations.
- Other authorized STN-LABZ activities.

Rictus may identify and report an SRT candidate within its assigned
mission.

Research or other authorized STN-LABZ activity may also identify
information warranting review by Security Research.

Identification of an SRT candidate does not establish the reported
condition as fact, establish an organizational threat, or authorize
further processing.


Candidate Threshold

An SRT candidate may be identified when available information provides
a reasonable basis to investigate a potential condition capable of
adversely affecting:

- Security.
- Integrity.
- Availability.
- Confidentiality.
- Operational or command authority boundaries.
- Safety.
- Infrastructure.
- Engineering systems.
- Autonomous systems.
- Communications.
- Space systems or missions.
- STN-LABZ operations or mission objectives.
- Other systems or interests within authorized STN-LABZ scope.

The existence of cybersecurity, space, AI, communications, or other
technically relevant subject matter alone does not establish an SRT
candidate.

Candidate identification establishes a research question or potential
condition requiring review.

It does not establish a threat determination.


Intelligence and Discovery Identity

Information retained through STN-LABZ intelligence or research activity
may receive a stable intelligence identifier before becoming an SRT.

Example:

INT-8C4F2A71

The intelligence identifier identifies the originating intelligence or
research object.

Assignment of an intelligence identifier does not:

- Establish an SRT.
- Establish a threat.
- Authorize Security Research.
- Establish organizational knowledge.
- Authorize publication.

When an SRT originates from an identified intelligence object, the SRT
record will preserve the originating intelligence identifier.


SRT Candidate

A condition identified for possible Security Research will be designated
as an:

SRT CANDIDATE

An SRT candidate will initially be treated as:

POTENTIAL — NOT ESTABLISHED

An SRT candidate may be reported for human review.

Where an intelligence identifier exists, candidate reporting may identify
the candidate by that identifier.

Example:

INT-8C4F2A71 — SRT CANDIDATE

The candidate remains potential until evidence and authorized human
review establish an applicable disposition.

Candidate status alone does not authorize Security Research activity.


Human-Controlled Progression

The SRT process is human-controlled.

No component, agent, module, research process, database, or successful
processing stage independently authorizes progression across an authority
boundary.

Applicable transitions require explicit human interaction.

Human interaction may include:

- Review.
- Approval.
- Rejection.
- Revision.
- Routing.
- Research authorization.
- Publication authorization.
- Distribution authorization.
- Other explicit authorized human action.

Automated systems may collect, process, validate, preserve, present, or
recommend information within their authorized scope.

Automation does not replace required human authority.

A successful processing stage establishes only that the applicable stage
completed successfully.

It does not independently authorize the next stage.


Security Research

Security Research evaluates SRT candidates routed or authorized for
security investigation.

Security Research may:

- Examine the originating observation or intelligence.
- Locate root or primary evidence.
- Collect supporting evidence.
- Collect contradictory evidence.
- Reproduce or test technical claims when authorized and appropriate.
- Identify affected systems or domains.
- Assess technical plausibility.
- Document unknowns.
- Assess potential impact.
- Recommend additional research.
- Document potential mitigation.
- Recommend an applicable disposition.

Security Research will preserve the distinction between:

- Observed fact.
- Reported claim.
- Technical evidence.
- Inference.
- Assessment.
- Unknown information.

Security Research does not independently create organizational policy or
approve information as organizational knowledge.


Security Research Threat Record

Each formal SRT will receive a stable identifier sufficient to uniquely
identify the research record.

The identifier format will support multiple SRT records without ambiguity.

Example:

SRT-YYYYMMDD-NNN

An SRT originating from an identified intelligence object will preserve
both identities.

Example:

Origin Intelligence ID: INT-8C4F2A71

SRT ID: SRT-20260818-001

The intelligence identifier and SRT identifier serve different purposes.

The intelligence identifier identifies the originating intelligence or
research object.

The SRT identifier identifies the formal Security Research record.


SRT Record Content

The SRT record will include, as applicable:


Origin

Identify the originating intelligence, research, engineering, operational,
or other authorized activity.

Where available, preserve the originating intelligence identifier.


Title / Research State

Identify the subject of the research and its current research state.


Threat

What potential security condition has been identified?


Observation

What has actually been observed, demonstrated, or reported?

Observation will remain distinguishable from interpretation or inference.


Potential Impact

How could the identified condition affect STN-LABZ systems, operations,
engineering, security, infrastructure, mission objectives, or the public?


Affected Domains

Applicable domains may include:

- Desktop / Laptop
- Server
- Network
- Space
- AI
- Communications
- Infrastructure
- Autonomous Systems
- Other affected systems or environments


Evidence

Preserve evidence supporting the SRT, including as applicable:

- Original discovery source.
- Root or primary source.
- Supporting references.
- Technical documentation.
- Research.
- Demonstrations.
- Incidents.
- Contradictory evidence.
- Other relevant evidence.

Provenance will be preserved sufficiently to allow later verification.


Assessment

Analysis and interpretation will remain distinguishable from established
facts.

Capability will not be interpreted as authority.

The existence of a technical capability does not establish that its use
was authorized, intended, exploitable, or applicable to STN-LABZ.


Unknowns

Information that cannot be established from available evidence will remain:

UNKNOWN

Unknown information will not be replaced with speculation.


Confidence

Confidence will be identified as:

- LOW
- MODERATE
- HIGH

Confidence describes the strength of the available assessment.

Confidence does not convert an unverified condition into established fact.


Recommended Research

Identify additional investigation, testing, evidence, or analysis required
to determine the condition more completely.


Potential Mitigation

Potential defensive or engineering measures may be documented for
research and review.

Potential mitigation is not policy unless separately approved through the
STN-LABZ policy process.


Human Actions

The record will preserve applicable human actions affecting progression,
including as appropriate:

- Candidate review.
- Routing to Security Research.
- Research authorization.
- Revision.
- Disposition.
- Publication authorization.
- Distribution authorization.

Records will identify the action and sufficient provenance to establish
that the applicable human authority boundary was crossed intentionally.


Human Review

SRT candidates require human review.

Human review may result in the candidate being:

- Approved for further research.
- Revised.
- Escalated.
- Routed.
- Closed.

Creation of an SRT candidate does not establish that:

- The reported condition is true.
- The condition is exploitable.
- The condition applies to STN-LABZ.
- The condition constitutes an established organizational threat.
- The condition requires policy.
- The condition has been approved as a research assignment.
- The condition has been approved for publication.
- The condition has been approved as organizational knowledge.

Human authorization is required before an SRT candidate becomes an
approved research assignment.


Disposition

An SRT may receive an applicable disposition including:

- MONITOR
- RESEARCH
- ESCALATE
- RECOMMEND POLICY
- CLOSED

Disposition will reflect the current authorized treatment of the SRT and
will not be interpreted as evidence beyond what the SRT record establishes.

Disposition does not independently authorize publication or distribution.


Publication

SRT research may produce information suitable for publication or
organizational use.

Publication requires human authorization.

Research completion does not independently authorize publication.

Before an SRT-derived artifact enters an approved organizational knowledge
path, the applicable publication, integrity, provenance, and human-review
requirements will be completed.

A published controlled artifact will receive the applicable content hash
or other required integrity record before authorized downstream use.

Publication does not erase the underlying SRT research history.


Distribution

Authorized published SRT material may be distributed to approved
STN-LABZ destinations.

Applicable destinations may include:

- Digit through the approved knowledge pipeline.
- The STN-LABZ SRT database.
- Other specifically authorized STN-LABZ systems or repositories.

Distribution requires the applicable human action and does not occur
merely because research or publication processing completed successfully.

Raw SRT candidates, unapproved assessments, and unresolved research will
not become approved Digit knowledge merely because they exist within the
SRT process.

The SRT database may preserve research and historical records according to
its authorized purpose.

Digit receives only information authorized through the applicable
organizational knowledge process.


Integrity and Provenance

Published or controlled SRT artifacts will preserve sufficient integrity
and provenance information to establish:

- Origin.
- Applicable intelligence identifier.
- Applicable SRT identifier.
- Evidence lineage.
- Human decisions.
- Publication state.
- Content integrity.
- Applicable downstream distribution.

Where a content hash is required, the hash identifies the applicable
content state and does not replace the permanent identity of the
underlying intelligence or SRT record.


Policy Boundary

An SRT does not create organizational policy.

An SRT may recommend policy when research identifies a condition requiring
organizational governance.

Policy creation, amendment, approval, or rejection remains a separate
human-governed process.

Potential mitigation documented within an SRT does not become mandatory
merely because it appears in the research record.


Authority Boundary

Security Research identifies and evaluates potential conditions.

Rictus may discover, collect, correlate, document, and report information
relevant to an SRT candidate within its assigned mission.

Other authorized STN-LABZ research and engineering activities may identify
and route potential SRT conditions for human review.

Rictus will not:

- Independently establish an SRT candidate as an organizational threat.
- Independently approve SRT research assignments.
- Independently approve organizational knowledge.
- Establish company policy.
- Bypass required human review.
- Expand its own authority.

Security Research will not independently authorize organizational
knowledge, publication, policy, or progression beyond its delegated
authority.

Digit will not treat unapproved SRT research or candidate information as
approved organizational knowledge.

Databases, pipelines, modules, and other automated systems will not treat
successful processing as human authorization.

Human authority remains the approval boundary.


Historical Record

SRT records will preserve supporting and contradictory evidence necessary
to understand how the assessment developed.

An SRT that is rejected, disproven, revised, superseded, or closed remains
part of the engineering and security research record.

Contrary evidence will not be removed merely because it weakens the
original hypothesis.

Applicable human decisions and state transitions will remain attributable
within the historical record.


Implementation Boundary

This policy defines governance and authority requirements.

Specific implementation mechanisms used to express human action are
defined separately by the applicable system or operational procedure.

Such mechanisms may include authenticated commands, administrative
interfaces, manual review actions, controlled-document approval, or other
authorized methods.

An implementation mechanism does not alter the human authority requirement.

For example, a future Rictus command may provide an operational mechanism
for requesting or authorizing an SRT action.

The existence of such a command does not give Rictus independent authority
to initiate, approve, publish, or distribute SRT research.
