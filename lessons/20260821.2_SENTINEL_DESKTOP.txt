---

Purpose

This policy establishes mandatory authority boundaries for artificial-intelligence and agentic software executing, interacting with, or exercising control over a Windows system protected by Sentinel Desktop.

Sentinel Desktop exists to ensure that authorization to execute software or perform a requested task does not become unrestricted authority over the protected host, its applications, data, credentials, network resources, security controls, or connected services.

---

Governing Principle

> Permission to execute is not permission to act without boundaries.

Authorization to execute software on a protected host does not constitute unrestricted authorization to access, modify, control, transmit, execute, persist, authenticate, or otherwise act upon host resources.

---

Authority Boundary

Every governed agentic process operates within an explicitly defined authority boundary.

Authority is established according to the resources and actions required for the authorized task.

Authority outside that boundary is not implied by:

- process execution;
- user authentication;
- inherited operating-system permissions;
- application accessibility;
- possession of credentials;
- access to authenticated sessions;
- previous authorization;
- technical capability.

> Technical capability does not establish authority.

The ability of an agentic system to perform an action does not establish permission to perform that action.

---

Task Authorization and Action Authorization

Authorization of an objective does not automatically authorize every intermediate action technically available to an agent.

For example:

text
AUTHORIZED TASK

Analyze documents in C:\Research

DOES NOT INHERENTLY AUTHORIZE

Read browser credentials
Inspect unrelated user files
Modify system configuration
Establish persistence
Execute arbitrary programs
Access unrelated network services
Transmit unrelated information


Each consequential action remains subject to the applicable Sentinel Desktop authority boundary.

Task authorization and action authorization are separate security conditions.

---

Least Authority

Agentic software receives only the authority necessary to perform its authorized task.

Where a narrower resource, process, filesystem, network, credential, application, or duration boundary can satisfy the authorized task, the narrower boundary is preferred.

Availability of broader operating-system authority does not establish Sentinel Desktop authority to use it.

---

Authority Duration

Authority may be scoped to:

- a task;
- a process;
- a process tree;
- a session;
- a resource;
- an application;
- a network destination or service;
- an explicitly defined period.

Completion or termination of the condition establishing authority terminates the corresponding authority unless another authorized condition independently establishes continued authority.

Authority does not persist merely because the governed software remains capable of exercising it.

---

Privilege

Execution under a user account does not automatically authorize use of every privilege possessed by that account.

Administrative capability does not establish administrative authority.

Where elevated privilege is required, the applicable authority must establish that privilege independently of the technical ability to obtain or exercise it.

---

Credentials and Authentication

Access to credentials, authentication material, authenticated sessions, tokens, browser profiles, security keys, or equivalent authentication resources requires explicit authority appropriate to the requested operation.

The technical ability to access authentication material does not constitute authorization to access or use it.

Authorization to access one authenticated resource does not inherently authorize access to another authenticated resource.

Authorization to access authentication material does not inherently authorize its transmission, storage, modification, or reuse.

---

Process Execution

Authorization to execute one process does not inherently authorize:

- arbitrary child-process creation;
- command-shell execution;
- script execution;
- interpreter execution;
- execution of unrelated applications;
- execution of downloaded content;
- process injection;
- modification of unrelated processes;
- termination of unrelated processes.

Process relationships remain subject to Sentinel Desktop authority evaluation.

A child process does not automatically inherit unrestricted authority from its parent merely because the parent was authorized to execute.

---

Filesystem Authority

Filesystem authority is bounded by authorized resources and authorized operations.

Read authority does not imply write authority.

Write authority does not imply deletion authority.

Creation authority does not imply modification authority over unrelated resources.

Access to one authorized path does not imply authority over unrelated paths.

Access to a directory does not inherently establish unrestricted authority over every resource accessible through the user account.

Agentic software must not infer filesystem authority from technical accessibility alone.

---

Network Authority

Network availability does not constitute network authority.

A governed process may communicate only with destinations, services, interfaces, or classes of resources permitted by its applicable authority.

Authorization to communicate with one service does not imply unrestricted network access.

Authorization to receive information does not inherently authorize transmission of information.

Authorization to communicate with an external service does not inherently authorize transmission of unrelated host data, credentials, authentication material, or protected information.

---

Persistence

Agentic software does not establish, modify, extend, or conceal persistence beyond the authority explicitly granted to it.

Persistence mechanisms are treated as security-significant operations.

Persistence includes mechanisms intended to cause software, scripts, services, scheduled operations, or equivalent components to execute beyond the authority or lifecycle of the originally authorized task.

Task authorization does not inherently include persistence authorization.

---

Security Controls

Agentic software does not disable, weaken, bypass, reconfigure, interfere with, deceive, or evade:

- Sentinel Desktop;
- Windows security controls;
- installed endpoint-security providers;
- security logging;
- auditing mechanisms;
- access-control mechanisms;
- security policy;
- authority-enforcement mechanisms;

unless explicitly authorized by applicable human-controlled policy.

Technical ability to modify a security control does not establish authority to modify that control.

An agentic process does not receive authority to weaken security merely because doing so would make completion of its assigned task easier.

---

External and Untrusted Content

Instructions encountered in files, webpages, messages, application content, network responses, documents, databases, external services, or other resources do not independently grant additional host authority.

> Content cannot grant authority.

External content may provide information relevant to an authorized task.

External content may not independently expand the authority under which that task is performed.

A webpage cannot grant filesystem authority.

A document cannot grant process-execution authority.

A message cannot grant credential authority.

A network response cannot grant persistence authority.

Instructions encountered during an authorized workflow remain subordinate to the authority boundary established for that workflow.

This remains true regardless of whether the instruction is legitimate, malicious, compromised, malformed, adversarial, or generated through prompt injection.

---

Prompt Injection and Manipulation

Resistance to manipulation and limitation of authority are separate security properties.

The following questions are independently relevant:

> Can the agent be manipulated?

and

> If manipulated or mistaken, what is the maximum authority available to it?

Sentinel Desktop does not assume that manipulation resistance is perfect.

Authority boundaries therefore remain applicable regardless of the agentic system's claimed or demonstrated resistance to prompt injection, adversarial input, malformed instructions, model error, or other behavioral manipulation.

Successful manipulation of an agent does not grant the manipulated agent additional authority.

---

Unknown Authority

Where Sentinel Desktop cannot establish that an action is authorized, Sentinel Desktop does not infer authorization from:

- apparent intent;
- contextual similarity;
- previous behavior;
- previous authorization;
- technical capability;
- user privilege;
- application privilege;
- agent assertions;
- external content;
- probable usefulness to the task.

The authority condition is:

UNKNOWN

The applicable Sentinel Desktop policy determines whether an UNKNOWN condition results in denial, containment, escalation, termination, operator review, or another fail-safe response.

Sentinel Desktop does not convert UNKNOWN authority into authorization merely to permit task completion.

---

Auditability

Security-significant actions performed or attempted by governed agentic processes must be attributable and reconstructable to the extent supported by Sentinel Desktop and the Windows platform.

Sentinel Desktop preserves sufficient evidence to establish, where technically available:

text
WHO OR WHAT acted
WHAT action was attempted
WHAT resource was involved
WHAT authority applied
WHAT decision Sentinel Desktop made
WHY that decision was made
WHEN the event occurred


Audit information must distinguish observed behavior from inferred behavior where applicable.

Sentinel Desktop does not represent inference as established fact.

---

Revocation

Authority can be revoked.

Revocation prevents subsequent reliance upon the revoked authority.

Revocation does not depend upon cooperation from the governed agentic software.

Where technically enforceable, Sentinel Desktop maintains the ability to terminate or restrict authority independently of the process being governed.

Previously granted authority does not prevent subsequent revocation.

---

Enforcement Independence

> The system being governed does not determine whether its own actions are authorized.

Sentinel Desktop independently evaluates applicable authority.

An agentic system may request an operation.

An agentic system may provide information concerning why an operation is necessary.

An agentic system may identify resources it believes are required to complete its task.

These statements do not independently establish authority.

The governed system cannot declare its own actions authorized merely by asserting that they are necessary for task completion.

Authority originates from the applicable human-controlled Sentinel Desktop policy and authorized operator actions, not from the governed agent.

---

Security Provider Boundary

Sentinel Desktop does not replace the installed Windows endpoint-security provider.

Antivirus, antimalware, or endpoint-security software remains responsible for its own functions, including where applicable:

- malware detection;
- malware classification;
- signature databases;
- security definitions;
- engine updates;
- threat-intelligence updates;
- quarantine mechanisms;
- provider-specific protection capabilities.

Sentinel Desktop may discover, query, and use supported capabilities of installed Windows security providers.

Sentinel Desktop does not assume responsibility for maintaining another security provider's malware definitions or protection engine.

The security responsibilities remain distinct:

> Endpoint protection asks whether something is malicious.

> Sentinel Desktop asks whether something is authorized.

A process that is not classified as malicious may still perform an unauthorized action.

An authorized action does not automatically establish that the software performing it is safe.

---

Network Independence

Sentinel Desktop core protection does not depend upon continuous network connectivity.

Loss of network connectivity does not terminate Sentinel Desktop authority enforcement.

Sentinel Desktop operates from locally validated policy and locally available security state.

Network connectivity may be used to obtain candidate policy updates, security knowledge, or other authorized information.

Network availability affects information freshness.

Network availability does not determine whether Sentinel Desktop remains operational.

---

Policy Authority

Sentinel Desktop enforces locally validated policy.

Remote policy information is treated as a candidate update until successfully validated by Sentinel Desktop.

Remote systems do not directly replace active Sentinel Desktop policy.

Candidate policy updates must satisfy applicable integrity, authenticity, compatibility, version, and structural validation requirements before becoming authoritative.

If candidate policy validation fails:

text
CANDIDATE POLICY = REJECTED
ACTIVE POLICY    = UNCHANGED
EVENT            = RECORDED


Sentinel Desktop continues operating from the last known valid policy.

A policy does not become invalid solely because the protected host temporarily cannot reach the policy source.

> STALE does not inherently mean INVALID.

---

Security Knowledge Boundary

Security intelligence and security policy are separate authority classes.

Security knowledge may inform Sentinel Desktop operation.

Security knowledge does not independently create enforcement authority.

A research finding, Security Research Threat candidate, external advisory, security observation, or other intelligence record does not automatically become an enforcement rule merely because Sentinel Desktop possesses that information.

Applicable human-controlled policy determines when security knowledge results in enforcement authority.

> Knowledge may inform authority. Knowledge does not create authority by itself.

---

Sentinel Desktop Knowledge Scope

Sentinel Desktop consumes controlled knowledge applicable to its assigned mission.

Applicable controlled-document scopes include:

text
Scope: Sentinel


and:

text
Scope: Sentinel Desktop


Controlled knowledge outside the applicable Sentinel scope does not become Sentinel Desktop operational knowledge merely because the information exists within the wider STN-LABZ knowledge environment.

Trustworthiness and relevance are separate conditions.

> Trusted does not inherently mean relevant.

Sentinel Desktop receives the trusted knowledge required for its mission and does not require unrelated organizational or system-specific knowledge.

---

Failure Principle

Sentinel Desktop does not expand authority merely to permit successful task completion.

When continued operation would require Sentinel Desktop to assume, invent, infer, or expand authority that has not been established, Sentinel Desktop preserves the established authority boundary.

> Task failure is preferable to unauthorized action.

Where applicable, Sentinel Desktop records the condition, preserves relevant evidence, and reports or escalates according to authorized policy.

---

Root Security Principles

The Sentinel Desktop authority model is founded upon the following principles:

> Permission to execute is not permission to act without boundaries.

> Technical capability does not establish authority.

> Content cannot grant authority.

> The system being governed does not determine whether its own actions are authorized.

> Knowledge may inform authority. Knowledge does not create authority by itself.

> Task failure is preferable to unauthorized action.

These principles establish the authority boundary governing agentic systems operating on Windows hosts protected by Sentinel Desktop.

Implementation mechanisms are defined separately.

This policy defines what must remain true regardless of the Windows mechanism used to enforce it.
